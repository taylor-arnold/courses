As in the previous homework, suppose that we are recording the attendance of the twenty students in our class across the twenty-five class meetings, now using three tables: student, with one row for each student and columns named name and major; meeting, with one row for each class meeting and columns named meeting, date, and topic; and attendance, with one row for each student at each meeting and columns named name, meeting, and present. Hand write the following.

  1. A data dictionary for the three tables, in the form the reading describes. For every column, give its name, its type, its units or its expected set of values, and any decision you had to make about how it is recorded. Then document the relationship between each pair of linked tables, naming the primary key on one end and the foreign key on the other.
  2. The reading recommends two ways to record a date. Write out how a meeting held at 9:30 in the morning on October 21st, 2026 would appear in the meeting table under each of them, and give one reason to prefer each. Then explain what is wrong with recording the same meeting as 10/21/26.
  3. One week you are out sick, and nobody records attendance at two of the meetings. Say exactly what should appear in the present cells of attendance for those meetings and why, following the reading's rule for missing values, and explain how a reader of the table can later tell the difference between a student who was absent and a student whose attendance was never recorded. Then say where a note explaining the two missing meetings should go, and name two places it should not go.
  4. Suppose that you photograph the whiteboard at the end of every class meeting and want to keep the photographs with the data. Say which table the photographs belong to and what column you would add to it, following the reading's advice for text and multimedia, and write out the value that column would hold for the third meeting. Then explain why the photograph itself does not go into the spreadsheet.
  5. The student table uses name as its primary key. Describe the situation in which name stops being a valid primary key, and say what the reading suggests doing about it. Then write out the first three rows of student and of attendance as they would look after the change, and explain why the change has to be made in both tables.
  6. A description of how the three tables and the data dictionary would be laid out in a single spreadsheet file, following the reading's guidelines and its worked example: how many sheets there are, what each one is named, what sits in cell A1 of each, and where the data dictionary goes. Then name one kind of formatting the reading allows and one it forbids, and say what format you would save the data in once the semester is over and why.
