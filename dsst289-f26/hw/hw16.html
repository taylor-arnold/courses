Homework 16

Due at Project Workshop I. This is the revised version of the schema you drafted in Homework 15, and it is the last thing you write before you start collecting. In class you will hand it to a partner whose job is to break it: to find the row they cannot record, the key that does not resolve, the category you forgot. The more of it you have written down, the more useful that will be. Your schema is locked at the end of class and collection begins on Monday.

  1. The revised schema. Every table, with its unit of observation, its primary key, and every foreign key marked along with the table it points to. Then every column, with its name, its type, and its units or its expected set of values. Check every name against the naming rules from the reading and note any you had to change.
  2. Three of the reading's rules, applied to your own tables. Point to a column that follows an external standard, say what the standard is and where you looked it up. Point to a variable you could have recorded as text and have recorded as a number instead. Point to one cell where you were tempted to record two things at once and kept the cell atomic instead, and say what the second thing is and where it went.
  3. Your missing-value convention. There will be hours you do not record. Say exactly what will appear in the file for those hours, and explain why. Then say how you will tell the difference, later, between an hour you spent doing nothing and an hour you failed to write down.
  4. Set up your spreadsheet. Describe how you will lay the tables out: which sheets, in what order, what goes in cell A1, and what formatting you will use to help yourself enter data without ever storing information in the formatting itself.
  5. Hand write the first four lines of your hours table exactly as they would look in the plain text of a CSV file, opened in a text editor: the header row and three rows of data, filled in with the hours you have already lived today.
  6. The two parts of your schema you are least sure about, written out as questions for your partner. These are what you will trade in class.
