The third exam is different to the first two. Because the focus of this unit has been creating data, you will be creating data for the exam. That is not very well-adapted to an in-class exam. Therefore, the exam will be a take-home data creation project with two parts. Both are due at the start of class on April 13th. Please come to class as well because we will have an open-ended workshop to get started on the final project.

Part 1: You will need to collect a dataset about a seven-day week in your life. I suggest starting at midnight on Monday, April 6th and ending one week later. You will create four tables in CSV format and upload them in a Box link that I will share with you. Here are the four tables you need to create

  • hours.csv — A dataset with one row for each hour of the week. Include column(s) to identify the hour (up to you how to do this) and record where you were and what activity you were doing at the top of the hour. Also record how many minutes during the hour you were doing that activity. Also include a column that records what class you were either working on or currently in at the top of the hour.
  • places.csv — This dataset will contain one row for each of the places that you have in your hours dataset. Make sure there is a primary key that links to the hours dataset. For each place, record the longitude and latitude (approximate) of the location. You can use Google maps (right click on the map) for this.
  • courses.csv — Contains one row for each course you are taking. Make sure there is a primary key pointing back to the hours dataset. Record three different metadata columns about the course (such as the difficulty; is it for you major/minor; whatever you want!).
  • activities.csv — Contains one row for each type of activity that you do (note: these can be broad, such as "studying" or "sleeping"). Make sure there is a primary key pointing back to the hours dataset. Record two metadata columns about each activity. Your choice about what those are.

You will get full credit if you follow the rules above and those in Chapter 5's reading about data creation. Note: If you are engaged in an activity that is not the kind of thing you would normally share in a classroom setting, please feel free to replace it with another one.

Part 2: Using (part of) the dataset that you produced for the first section, hand draw a fun, creative visualization of your week in some form. For inspiration, check out the Dear Data website. It does not have to use all (or even more than one) of the tables. Just have fun with it and bring your visualization to class.
