\begin{center}
\textbf{DSST389: Advanced Data Science --- Fall 2026}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.5cm}

\textbf{Website}: \texttt{https://taylor-arnold.github.io/courses/dsst389-f26}

\bigskip

\textbf{Instructor:}
This course is taught by Prof.~Taylor Arnold (\texttt{taylor.arnold@richmond.edu}).
I am happy to hear from you about anything to do with the course material, the
readings, the homework, or the project.

\bigskip

\textbf{Topics:}
Advanced approaches to the analysis of data, working with the kinds of data that do
not arrive as a tidy table: spatial data, data pulled from web APIs, time series,
text, networks, and images.

\bigskip

\textbf{Format:}
The semester is broken into three units, each ending with an in-class exam, followed
by a final project that runs through the end of the term. Class meetings are hands-on
and interactive. Please bring a computer, pencil, and something to write on to each
class. Class materials can be found on the course website.

\bigskip

\textbf{Software:}
We will be programming in the open-source Python programming language.
Classwork can be run through Google's Colab environment in a web browser using
a University email login. Each student will also have a personal shared Box folder
that will be used to submit and return assignments and grades. Other hardware
and software may be used for some data collection tasks; all of these will
be provided or freely accessible.

\bigskip

\textbf{Reading and Homework:}
Most class meetings will have a short reading and homework task posted on the course
website. Homework is due at the start of class. It is not graded and carries no points,
but we will use it in class, and the exams and the final project are built directly on
top of it. Coming to class without it means sitting out the part of the class that
matters most.

\medskip

Please write your homework out by hand, on a fresh sheet of paper. Do not type it, print
it, or fill in the margins of a printed handout. The exams are hand-written, and writing
the code out yourself is most of the reason the homework helps. From time to time I
will collect the homework at the start of class. When I do, I am only checking that
you have done it and made a genuine attempt at it. I am not grading it, and an honest
wrong answer is exactly what I want to see.

\bigskip

\textbf{Attendance:}
At the beginning of each class meeting you will complete a brief class form used to
record attendance. \textit{Do not fill out the form if you are not present.} Your
attendance grade is the percentage of class meetings you attend, with your first two
missed meetings forgiven: it is computed as the number of meetings you attended divided
by the total number of meetings minus two, capped at 100. The two forgiven absences are
there to cover routine illness and other unavoidable circumstances, so you do not need
to explain them to me. If you find yourself needing to miss more than two meetings,
contact me as soon as you can so that we can make appropriate arrangements.

\bigskip

\textbf{External Speakers:}
You are required to attend at least two external talks over the course of the semester.
These are a required component of the course but are not associated with a numerical
grade. A list of talks is posted on the course website; any of those will count, no
questions asked. You may also count any other on-campus talk with a data science
component, which I encourage --- there are more of these than I can list. For a talk
that is not on the list, you will be asked to write a slightly longer justification
explaining what the data science component was. Attendance is recorded through the talk
form linked on the course website. Contact me as soon as possible if you are unable to
attend two talks so that we can discuss appropriate arrangements.

\bigskip

\textbf{Exams:}
There are three in-class exams, given on the dates indicated on the course website.
They consist of short, hand-written blocks of code, along with a small number of short
written definitions. A study guide for each exam, listing exactly what it covers and
giving practice questions with answers, will be posted on the course website at least
one week ahead of time. There is no final exam.

\bigskip

\textbf{Final Project:}
The final project is launched in mid-October and runs through the end of the semester.
You will define a population of Wikipedia pages, collect it yourself through the
MediaWiki and Wikimedia APIs, document it, check it, and analyze it with the temporal,
textual, and network methods from the second half of the course. The full description,
including the schedule and the deliverables, is posted on the course website. Two class
meetings are set aside as project workshops, and the last three meetings of the semester
are given over to presentations.

\bigskip

\textbf{Artificial Intelligence:}
You may use generative AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, and so on) for work done
outside of class, as long as you document that you used them. Documentation means a short
note attached to the work saying which tool you used and what you used it for. I am not
trying to catch anyone out. I ask because knowing when and how people reach for these
tools is useful to me in teaching the course. Undocumented use is a violation of the
honor code.

\medskip

Nothing we do in class requires AI. The in-class work, the exams, and the hand-written
homework are all meant to be done without it. If you find that you need a model to get
through them, please come talk to me, because that usually means some piece of the
material has not landed yet and I would rather hear about it early. You are also
responsible for anything you submit under your name, including text or code that a model
wrote for you.

\bigskip

\textbf{Getting Help:}
We will usually have time in class to answer questions about the course
material. If questions arise outside of class, please feel free to send
these by email. I am happy to schedule office hours for any extended questions
or personal concerns. Because I know everyone has a busy schedule, I offer
office hours primarily by appointment.

\bigskip

The final grade for the semester is computed as follows:

\medskip
