Disability and Personal Accommodations

[← back]

The course is built so that its default structure already works for a wide range of students, and much of what an accommodation letter asks for is something this class does for everyone anyway. Over the past several years there has been a significant increase in disability accommodation requests, which is why I have written out my responses to the most common ones below rather than handling each one privately. Two things follow from that. You can see what to expect here before you ask, and you can see that most of these provisions are open to the whole class, whether or not you have a piece of paper saying you need them. If you do have a disability accommodation notice, send it to me and we will sort out the details. If your situation is not covered below, or if something here does not fit what you need, write to me and we will figure out what will let you do your best work.

Extra Testing Time. The three in-class exams are written to take no more than 50 minutes of the 75-minute class period, and many students finish in 30. If your notice gives you time and a half, that comes to 75 minutes on a 50-minute exam, which is the whole period: start when everyone else does and keep working after the rest of the class has left. Nothing needs to be arranged ahead of time. If you need more than the full period, we can continue in my office right after class, since the room may be in use.

Flexible Attendance. Attendance is 20% of the course grade. It is the number of meetings you attended divided by the total number of meetings minus two, capped at 100, so the first two absences are already forgiven for every student with no explanation asked for. An accommodation for additional absences raises that number: whatever count your notice states is added to the two everyone gets, and the denominator changes to match. Nothing else about the grade changes. I still encourage you to come to every class you can, since the exams and the final project are built directly on what we do in the room. The requirement to attend two external talks is separate from the attendance grade; if getting to two of them is a problem, tell me early and we will find something that works.

Deadlines and Assignments. Homework is not graded and carries no points, so there is no late penalty and no due-date extension to negotiate. We do use it in class, and turning up without it means sitting out the part of the meeting that matters most, so it is worth doing even when it is late. The dates that actually bind are the three exams and the final project deliverables. The project's collection window opens at Project Workshop I on October 28th, once everyone's population is locked, and your collected dataset is due at Workshop II on November 18th; that schedule is the same for everyone and cannot be moved, because the whole class collects at once. If that window is a problem for you, come talk to me before the workshop and we will adapt what you collect rather than when you collect it.

Testing Space and Method. Exams are hand-written blocks of code on paper. If your notice calls for typing instead of handwriting, or for a quiet or reduced-distraction room, you are welcome to reserve a slot at the testing center for the exam date. Let me know at least a week ahead so that I can get the exam over to them in time.

Notes and Recording. In any class where note-taking is permitted, which is nearly all of them, any student may use a laptop, tablet, or audio recorder for their own private use. You do not need a formal accommodation for this, and recordings are for you and should not be shared. Exams are the exception, since they are written by hand on paper.

Availability of Materials. Everything you need is on the course website: the readings, the homework, the slides, the project description, and a study guide before each exam with practice questions and answers. Nothing is distributed only in class and nothing is taken down, so an accommodation for copies of course materials is already satisfied by default.

Personal Circumstances. If a serious personal or family issue comes up during the semester, tell me as soon as you can. You do not need a formal accommodation for this. We can work out a way for you to finish the course that also leaves you room to deal with what is happening outside of class.
