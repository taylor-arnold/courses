This reading finishes the chapter with four operations that change the geometries themselves: buffering, dissolving, exploding, and simplifying. On a sheet of paper, hand write answers to the following. A few sentences apiece is enough.

  1. Buffering every metro center by 100 kilometers produces discs that pile into a solid band along the eastern seaboard and sit alone out west. Say what that map shows about the country that the point map only hinted at. The discs also overlap, and the reading passes over what the overlaps mean. Say what it means for a piece of ground to fall inside two discs, and why that would be a problem if you buffered the metros and then added up the population they cover.
  2. The reading calls dissolve the spatial counterpart of group_by and agg. Say which part of dissolve corresponds to the grouping, which part corresponds to the aggregation, and what it does that has no counterpart in an ordinary aggregation. The example sums the state areas within each region. Explain why summing is right for area but would be wrong for the median household income column in the metro table, even though both are just numbers.
  3. Calling explode on the states turns Hawaii into one row per island and copies the state area onto every one of them. Say what the unit of observation is before the explode and what it is afterwards. Then go through the columns of the exploded table and say which ones still mean what they claim to mean and which ones are now false, and say what you have to recompute to fix them.
  4. The reading says to simplify for display rather than for analysis, because each geometry is simplified on its own and neighboring states can end up with small gaps or overlaps along a shared border. Explain what that would do to the touches join you thought about in the last homework, and say whether you would get an error or a wrong answer. Then look at the four failures in the best practices section and say which one you think you are most likely to commit without noticing, and why.
