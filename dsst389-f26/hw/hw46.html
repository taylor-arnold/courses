[← back]

This reading covers four more ways to build a network out of the same corpus. Two of the questions ask you to build one by hand.

  1. Four outside pages link to authors as follows — page 1 to A, B, C; page 2 to A, B; page 3 to B, C; page 4 to C, D — so write the co-citation edge list and draw the network keeping only edges with a count of 2 or more.
  2. Suppose a fifth page is a list article linking to all four authors, and say what it does to your edge list and why the reading therefore thresholds the count.
  3. For five documents with the pairwise distances A–B 0.20, A–C 0.50, A–D 0.90, A–E 1.00, B–C 0.30, B–D 0.80, B–E 0.95, C–D 0.40, C–E 0.85, D–E 0.35, build the symmetric k = 2 nearest-neighbor network, keeping an edge only when two documents are mutually nearest.
  4. Say why closeness centrality is unavailable once the network is directed.
  5. Say what you would conclude about an author who is central in the page-link network but peripheral in the textual-distance network.

Solutions

1. The co-citation edge list for the four outside pages, and the network with a count of 2 or more.

Each outside page contributes one co-citation for every pair of authors it
links to:

  page 1 (A, B, C):  A-B, A-C, B-C
  page 2 (A, B):     A-B
  page 3 (B, C):     B-C
  page 4 (C, D):     C-D

Counting the pairs gives the edge list:

  doc_id  doc_id2  count
  A       B        2
  A       C        1
  B       C        2
  C       D        1

Keeping only counts of 2 or more leaves A-B and B-C:

  A --- B --- C

D has no remaining edges, so it drops out of the network entirely, the same
way the three unlinked authors drop out of the reading's link network.

2. What a fifth page, a list article linking to all four authors, does to the edge list, and why the reading thresholds the count.

The list adds 1 to the count of every pair among the four authors, which is
all 6 pairs:

  doc_id  doc_id2  count
  A       B        3
  A       C        2
  A       D        1
  B       C        3
  B       D        1
  C       D        2

With the same cutoff of 2, the network gains A-C and C-D. Those edges exist
only because a single list mentioned all four authors. A list says the pages
share a category and nothing more specific than that. A list of all 75
authors in the corpus would add 75 * 74 / 2 = 2,775 pairs at once, joining
everyone to everyone.

The threshold asks for a pair to be co-cited by several different pages before
it becomes an edge. One list, or a few, cannot push a pair over a high cutoff
like the reading's count greater than 6, while pairs that many pages discuss
together still pass. The example also shows the limit: with a cutoff of 2,
one list was enough to add edges, so the cutoff has to be high enough relative
to how many list-like pages link into the corpus.

3. The symmetric k = 2 nearest-neighbor network for five documents.

First, each document's two nearest neighbors:

  A:  B (0.20), C (0.50)
  B:  A (0.20), C (0.30)
  C:  B (0.30), D (0.40)       A is third at 0.50
  D:  E (0.35), C (0.40)
  E:  D (0.35), C (0.85)

Then keep a pair only when each is in the other's list:

  A-B  yes  (A lists B, B lists A)
  A-C  no   (A lists C, but C does not list A)
  B-C  yes
  C-D  yes
  C-E  no   (E lists C, but C does not list E)
  D-E  yes

The network is a chain:

  A --- B --- C --- D --- E

No node has degree above k = 2. C is on the list of four documents, and it
would be a hub in a one-sided nearest-neighbor network. Here it keeps only
the two neighbors it chose itself.

4. Why closeness centrality is unavailable once the network is directed.

Closeness is built from the shortest-path distance between a node and every
other node. In a directed network, a path has to follow the arrows. That
causes two problems.

First, many nodes cannot reach many others. A page with no outgoing links
within the corpus reaches no one, and following arrows out of most pages
reaches only part of the network. Those distances are undefined, so the sum of
distances cannot be computed.

Second, distance is no longer the same in both directions. The distance from
X to Y can be 1 while the distance from Y to X is 4 or undefined. "How close
is this node to everyone?" splits into two questions, how easily it reaches
others and how easily others reach it, and a single closeness score does not
say which one it answers. So the reading's directed node table leaves it out
and keeps in-degree, out-degree, eigenvector, and betweenness, which have
directed versions.

5. What to conclude about an author who is central in the page-link network but peripheral in the textual-distance network.

There is no single correct answer, but a good one starts from what each
network records. The link network records which pages Wikipedia editors
link to each other. The distance network records how similar the vocabulary
of the pages is. An author central in the first and peripheral in the second
is a common reference point in other articles, but their own article is
written in words that the other articles do not share.

One plausible reading: the author is famous enough that editors mention them
across many periods and genres, as an influence or a point of comparison, while
their own page is full of names that belong to them alone, such as the titles
of their works, their characters, and the people around them. Shakespeare's
page, for example, could be linked from nearly everywhere and still be far
from other pages in TF-IDF terms because of all its play titles.

Both facts are about Wikipedia, not about the author's writing. Before drawing
a conclusion, check the page's top TF-IDF terms to see what makes it distant,
and check its length and number of outgoing links, since a long page that
links to many others can look central for that reason alone.
