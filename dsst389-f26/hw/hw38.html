This reading finishes the temporal chapter with truncation, window functions on real timestamps, durations, rolling windows, and joins on time. On a sheet of paper, hand write answers to the following.

  1. Say why truncating a timestamp, which throws information away, is the point rather than a cost.
  2. A reversion is flagged whenever an article's size equals its size two edits ago, so say how that rule could flag an edit that is not a reversion.
  3. Explain what a five-row rolling mean is actually averaging when two consecutive edits are forty seconds apart in one stretch of the series and eight months apart in another.
  4. Say why strategy="nearest" would be the wrong choice for an asof join asking whether edits follow attention.
  5. Say why the table pairing authors who were alive at the same time would tell you nothing if it were built from the 376 Victorians instead of these 75 authors.
