Homework 43

[← back]

This reading runs the same pipeline over the French Wikipedia pages for the same 75 authors. Hand write answers to the following.

  1. The French pages use more determiners, which the reading calls a linguistic rather than an editorial difference, so explain the distinction.
  2. Say what makes the English and French document vectors incomparable, in terms of what the axes of each space are.
  3. Say what you are actually comparing when you put the two UMAP plots side by side.
  4. Give one explanation for the French projection grouping the authors differently that has nothing to do with editors, and say what you would check to rule it out.

Solutions

1. Why the higher rate of determiners in French is a linguistic difference and not an editorial one.

A linguistic difference comes from the grammar of the language. An editorial
difference comes from choices the editors made about what to write.

French grammar requires an article before nearly every noun, including many
places where English uses none. English writes "poetry was his first love,"
and French writes "la poésie fut son premier amour." Any French text has
more determiners than its English counterpart, whether it is a Wikipedia
article, a novel, or a recipe. The gap would be there even if the French
editors had translated the English pages word for word.

So the higher rate tells us about French and nothing about how French editors
see these authors. An editorial difference would be something the editors could
have written otherwise: which works they mention, how much space they give to
an author's life, which people and places appear. Comparisons between the two
editions have to look past the grammatical differences to find those.

2. What makes the English and French document vectors incomparable.

Each axis of the space is a lemma, and the two spaces are built from different
lemmas. The English vectors have axes such as "poem," "novel," and "write,"
and the French vectors have axes such as "poème," "roman," and "écrire."
Nothing in the method says that "poem" and "poème" are the same axis, so an
English page and a French page about the same poet share almost no
coordinates.

The vocabularies are also filtered separately. max_df=0.5 is applied within
each corpus, so the set of terms kept, and so the set of axes, is decided by
each collection on its own terms. If you computed the angle between an English
vector and a French one, it would be close to pi / 2 for nearly every pair.
The few shared axes would be names spelled the same way in both languages.
That distance would measure the difference between the languages, not between
the pages.

3. What you compare when you put the two UMAP plots side by side.

You compare the structure of the two collections: which authors are grouped
together and which sit apart in each edition. Each plot is built from its own
space, so a point's position on one plot has no meaning on the other.

The coordinates themselves are arbitrary. UMAP's axes have no names, and a
projection can come out rotated or flipped without changing what it says. An
author being in the upper left in English and the lower right in French means
nothing. What can be compared is the neighborhoods: does Wordsworth sit near
Coleridge in both, do the Brontës stay together, does an author move from the
poets to the novelists? Remember too that the French plot has 73 points
and the English one 75, because two authors have no French page.

4. An explanation for different French groupings that has nothing to do with editors, and how to rule it out.

Several answers work. A good one names a cause in the data or the pipeline,
not in the editors' views, and then names a specific check.

One explanation is page length. If many French pages are short, each vector is
built from a few dozen lemmas, and the distances between them are noisy. The
groupings would then reflect chance overlaps in a small vocabulary, and a
different grouping would say nothing about how French editors see the
authors. To check, count the alphabetic tokens per page in anno_fr and compare
them with the English counts:

(
    anno_fr
    .filter(c.is_alpha)
    .group_by(c.doc_id)
    .agg(n_words = pl.len())
    .sort(c.n_words)
)

If the authors whose position changes are mostly the ones with short French
pages, length is a likely cause. If they have pages as long as their English
ones, it is not.

Other good answers:

  - The French spaCy model tags and lemmatizes differently, or less
    accurately, so the same content produces different terms. Check by
    reading the lemmas for a few pages and looking for errors.
  - UMAP is random, and two runs on the same data can group points
    differently. Check by running the English and French projections several
    times and seeing which groupings hold up every time.
  - The corpora differ: 73 documents against 75 changes N and every
    document frequency. Check by dropping the same two authors from the
    English corpus and rebuilding its plot.
