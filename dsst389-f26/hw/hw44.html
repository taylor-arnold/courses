Assigned October 14th and due at Project Workshop I on October 28th. The task is to choose the population of Wikipedia pages for your final project and write down how it is defined. Read the project description before you start. In class you will hand your definition to a partner whose job is to break it: to name a page your rule lets in that does not belong, and one it keeps out that does. Populations are locked at the end of class and collection begins that afternoon.

You have two weeks because the choice of population is the decision this project cannot recover from. Pick a candidate in the first week, look at its pages, and be willing to throw it away and pick another. Unlike the other homeworks, type your answers rather than hand writing them, so that they can be submitted through Box as part of your final project. Bring a copy to class as well, since you will work from it in the workshop.

  1. What the pages are about, in a phrase, and why you want to work with them.
  2. The population, written as a rule. One or two sentences, precise enough that somebody else could follow them and arrive at the same set of pages: which language edition, which categories or which source list or which Wikidata criteria, whether you descend into subcategories, and what you do with pages that are lists, disambiguations, or redirects.
  3. How many pages you expect the rule to produce, and how you know. The project calls for 75 to 250 pages. If your estimate falls outside that range, say how you will change the rule.
