This reading finishes the corpus: the links between articles, the revision histories, the daily page views, the language links, and a second corpus of 75 authors built from a curated list. On a sheet of paper, hand write answers to the following. A few sentences apiece is enough.

  1. Each row of the links table is a link from one author's article to some other page, and the network chapter will treat these as the edges of a graph. Charlotte Brontë's article links to Elizabeth Gaskell's, who wrote her biography. Say what a link like that is actually evidence of, and be careful here: the edge was put there by a Wikipedia editor in the last twenty years, not by either novelist. Then say what the reading gains by storing the full links table and treating links_internal as a view derived from it.
  2. The page views come from a second API with a different shape, where the request is built out of path segments rather than a dictionary of parameters. The reading fixes the window to the 2025 calendar year instead of asking for the last 365 days, and it explains why. Give that reason. Then say what a daily count of people reading the Emily Brontë article in 2025 is a measurement of, and name a question about Victorian novels that it cannot answer no matter how carefully it was collected.
  3. The first corpus has 376 novelists born within a few decades of each other, and the reading builds a second corpus of 75 authors spanning nine centuries because some questions are unanswerable on the first one. Give the example it uses and explain why the larger corpus fails at it. Then say what the second corpus costs: the list was typed by a person, and the birth years, death years, and gender columns came from no API at all. Say what has to be written down about that table before anyone else can trust it.
  4. Two of the 75 curated titles did not match an article directly. One was a typo and one was a real Wikipedia redirect, and the reading says the dangerous part is that both would have failed silently. Explain what "silently" means here and what the corpus would have looked like if nobody had checked. Then consider the three things that make this snapshot reproducible: the manifest, the requests cache, and the revision IDs. Say what each one lets you recover, and which one you would keep if you could only have one.
